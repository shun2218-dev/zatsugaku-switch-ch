title: Why do eyes glow red in photos? (The "red-eye effect")
answer: Those red eyes are actually showing you the inside of your own body.
description: |-
  When you take a flash photo in a dark place, people's pupils can glow red. This "red-eye effect" happens because the light bounces off the blood vessels at the back of the eye. Here's a simple explanation of how it works.

  ──────────
  ▼ Note: How red-eye reduction works

  The method shown in the video, a quick flash fired before the real one to make the pupils smaller (a pre-flash), is the basic way red-eye reduction works.
  Since digital cameras came along, many cameras also use a second method alongside it: after the photo is taken, the camera automatically detects red eyes and corrects the image.
  What "red-eye reduction" means varies by manufacturer, so check your camera's manual for details.
