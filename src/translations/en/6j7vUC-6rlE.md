### The window is "the coldest surface in the room"

The air in the whole room isn't freezing, so why is the window the only thing that fogs up? Because a window is a thin panel directly connected to the outside air.

In the Japan Meteorological Agency's forecasting terminology, the dew point is defined as "the temperature at which water vapor in the air condenses and forms dew." Whether condensation forms depends on whether a surface's temperature falls below the dew point.

Walls have insulation inside them, but a single pane of glass provides almost no insulation. So the window glass is the first thing in the room to drop below the dew point, and that's where the droplets gather. The reason the window gets wet and the walls don't isn't a difference in temperature but a **difference in insulation**.

Double windows and double-pane glass help with condensation because they keep the indoor-side glass above the dew point.

### Why breathing on glass fogs it instantly

The "breathe on it and it fogs up white instantly" example from the video is a case where it's the dew point that's shifting.

Your breath is warmed to nearly body temperature and holds a lot of water vapor. In other words, it's **air with a very high dew point**. Blow it on cold glass and the gap between the dew point and the surface temperature jumps, so a large amount of droplets forms in a flash.

That's why room air takes a while to slowly fog up a window, while your breath does it right away.

### Below 0°C, it becomes "frost"

According to the Japan Meteorological Agency's "Guide to Meteorological Observation," at 0°C (32°F) or below, the temperature at which air becomes saturated with respect to ice is called the **frost point**, because frost forms instead of dew.

On a really cold morning, the inside of a window sometimes has white patterns instead of water droplets. Those aren't frozen droplets; they're water vapor that attached directly as ice. They grow into feather-like shapes because ice crystals have set directions in which they extend.

### Fog is a sign of "too much water vapor in the room"

Looked at another way, window fog is an indicator that tells you the dew point of your room.

If your windows are dripping wet, it means the room's dew point is higher than the window's surface temperature. Just heating the room won't lower the dew point, so your only options are to get the water vapor itself out (ventilate) or dehumidify.

If you leave condensation sitting there, it can cause mold on window frames and walls, so ventilation is the real fix rather than wiping. Drying laundry indoors and running a humidifier both raise the dew point, so on days when the fogging is bad, cutting back on them helps.
