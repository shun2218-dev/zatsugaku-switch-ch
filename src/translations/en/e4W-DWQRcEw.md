### The test is defined by hard numbers

First, how much fitness is actually required? An article from the JFA (Japan Football Association) lays out the specific test for referees assigned to the J.League, Japan's professional league.

> The events differ for referees and assistant referees. To take a J1 referee as an example, one is a short-distance run called the sprint. You run 40m six times in a row, with a set interval between each. If all six aren't under 6 seconds, you fail.

**Six 40m sprints, every one in 6 seconds or less.** Go over on even one and you fail.

Then there's the second event.

> In the test called the interval run, which checks cardiopulmonary function, you run 75m in 15 seconds and walk 25m in 18 seconds. That's one rep, and you do 40 in a row.

Run 75m, walk 25m. That's one rep, done **40 times**. That's 4,000m in total.

> That's right. On a 400m track, that's 10 laps. For both the 40m sprint and the interval run, if you can't make the time on even one rep, you fail. You won't receive assignments.

### No pass, no matches

This is the heart of the system.

> But really, being able to run this is a given. To begin with, we can't entrust a J.League match to a referee who doesn't pass this fitness test. These are by no means easy numbers, you know? (laughs). But being able to clear them is what makes you a J.League referee. Clearing everything is the minimum requirement.

"Clearing everything is the minimum requirement." In other words, every referee standing on a J.League pitch has passed this test.

The test is held twice a year. Here's a 2014 JFA article:

> The fitness test for Class 1 and Women's Class 1 referees is held twice a year: before the season starts and during the season.
> Referees who do not pass this fitness test cannot receive assignments.

It's held mid-season too. Passing once before the season starts isn't enough.

### Held to the same standard as players

How fast is 40m in 6 seconds? Running 40m in 6.0 seconds works out to 24 km/h. That's not specialist sprinter territory, but it's a level where you can sprint on equal footing with soccer players.

Referees can keep running for 90 minutes not because of grit or habit. It's because they train like players and get measured like players.

The figure that [referees run about 10km per match](/trivia/HA9S5zn0TjA/) rests on this foundation of fitness.

### Why do they need to run?

Law 5 of the Laws of the Game defines the referee's job.

> Each match is controlled by a referee who has full authority to enforce the Laws of the Game in connection with the match.

To control a match, you have to see it. If you can't see it, you can't judge it.

The accuracy of a call is directly tied to your distance and angle from the play. A foul seen from 20m away and a foul seen from 5m to the side give you very different amounts of information. That's why referees keep moving close to the ball. If they can't move, they can't get a good angle.

In other words, a referee's fitness is the foundation of fairness. A referee who can't run makes worse calls.

### It's not that they don't get tired

To clear up a misconception: referees do get tired. They're not running "without tiring."

What they're good at is **pacing**. Players hit top speed when the ball is in play near them and walk to rest the rest of the time. Referees are the same. They aren't sprinting all the time; they switch between walking, jogging, and dashing.

The interval run is designed to measure exactly this. Run 75m, walk 25m, 40 times. It's a re-creation of how a match actually moves. It doesn't measure the ability to run flat out nonstop, but **the ability to recover during a short rest after running, and then run again**.

The "set interval" between the six 40m sprints exists for the same reason. Being fast on the first one alone means nothing; you have to run the sixth just as fast.

### Women's Class 1 and lower classes have standards too

The standards differ by class. According to the 2014 article, the sprint was 6 × 6.4 seconds for men's Class 1 and 6 × 6.8 seconds for Women's Class 1. The interval run was 20 reps of a 150m run + 50m walk, at 30.0 and 40.0 seconds for men's Class 1, and 35.0 and 45.0 seconds for Women's Class 1.

According to a JFA information page, even Class 3 certification includes an endurance interval run (75m + 25m) and a speed sprint (40m × 6), with all six sprints required to be within 6.9 seconds.

The numbers can change by year and by how the rules are applied, so anyone taking the test should check the latest standards from their own association. But the basic structure stays the same: every class has a fitness test. To hold the whistle, you first have to be able to run.
